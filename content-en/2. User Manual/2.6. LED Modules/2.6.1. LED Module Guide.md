> [!abstract] Information on the basic parts of LED modules, pixels, pitch, layout, scan methods, and the differences between indoor and outdoor modules
#### 1. Basic parts of an LED module

An LED module has many LED elements. With LED modules, you can build displays of many sizes and shapes. The basic parts are:

- LED element: a semiconductor element that emits light. RGB LED elements are used most often.
- PCB (Printed Circuit Board): the board that holds the LED elements and other electronic parts.
- Driver IC: an integrated circuit that controls the current of the LED elements.
- Power supply: supplies the power that the LED module needs.
- Case and connectors: protect the module and connect it to other modules and the controller.

#### 2. Pixels

A pixel is the smallest unit of an LED module. One pixel is usually one RGB LED element. Each pixel can show many colors, and many pixels together make an image. The higher the pixel density, the more detailed the image.

#### 3. Pitch

Pitch is the distance between two adjacent pixels. It is measured from the center of a pixel to the center of the adjacent pixel, in millimeters (mm). The smaller the pitch, the higher the pixel density, and the sharper and more detailed the screen.

- Narrow pitch: a sharp display at a short viewing distance.
- Wide pitch: good for viewing from far away, and lower cost.

#### 4. Layout

A basic LED module has 16x16 pixels. This is called "1 row × 1 column". The layout depends on how you place the modules horizontally and vertically.

- 1 row × 1 column (16x16 pixels): the most basic unit, with 256 pixels in total
- More columns: add one module horizontally to add a column (example: 1 row × 2 columns is 16x32 pixels)
- More rows: add one module vertically to add a row (example: 2 rows × 1 column is 32x16 pixels)
- Combined layout: place several modules horizontally and vertically (example: 4 rows × 4 columns is 64x64 pixels)

#### 5. Scan methods

The scan method is how data is sent in an LED display. The common scan methods are static scan and dynamic scan. Dynamic scan is divided into 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, and so on. Choose the scan method by the use and requirements of the display.

- 1/16 scan: divides the module into 16 parts and sends data one line at a time
    - Keeps the brightness while it uses less power
    - Used mainly in high-resolution displays
- 1/8 scan: divides the module into 8 parts and sends data one part at a time
    - Balances power consumption and brightness
    - Good for displays that need both resolution and brightness
- 1/4 scan: divides the whole module into 4 parts and sends data one part at a time
    - Uses more power, but gives a brighter display
    - Good for large displays where brightness is important
- Static (1/1 scan): turns on all LEDs at the same time
	- Sends data to each pixel at the same time
	- Gives the highest brightness and sharpness, but uses much power and makes much heat
- For the scan method, see [[3.16. Checking the LED Module Scan Method]]


#### 6. Differences between indoor and outdoor modules

LED modules are indoor or outdoor modules, by where you use them.

- Indoor LED modules:
    - High resolution and clear colors.
    - Low water and dust protection rating (IP rating).
    - Optimized for indoor lighting.
    - Usually a brightness of 800~1500 cd/m².
- Outdoor LED modules:
    - High brightness, so that they are easy to see even in strong sunlight.
    - High water and dust protection rating (IP65 or higher).
    - Durable, so they work reliably outdoors.
    - Usually a brightness of 4500 cd/m² or higher.
